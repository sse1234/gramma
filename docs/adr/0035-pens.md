# 35. Pens: the reader's marking colors, strengths and styles

Date: 2026-10-04

## Status

Accepted. Extends ADR 0023 (annotations); the mark model, its anchors
and its sync are unchanged.

## Context

ADR 0023 gave marks eight fixed HCL washes. Field use asked for three
things: colors of the reader's own choosing, marks that distract less
than a full wash, and an underline instead of a marker pen. A fourth
wish was a note on a verse that leaves the text alone, found again by
a small sign at the verse.

A mark references its color by index and travels through the synced
user store. Whatever replaces the fixed palette has to keep a mark
looking the same on every device, and must not touch the typesetting:
ADR 0023's rule that washes paint behind the Knuth–Plass runs and never
move a word stands.

## Decision

**Eight pens, the reader's.** A pen is a hue (HCL, degrees), a
strength (its opacity over the paper, 20 to 100 %), and a style: wash
or underline. Marks keep their color index; it now names a pen. The
pens live as `pen/<index>` keys in the synced user store beside the
marks, so a mark made on the phone looks the same on the desk. A pen
never written is the standard pen of ADR 0023 at full strength, so
existing marks look as they did.

**Where pens are set.** Settings → Appearance shows the eight pens;
tapping one opens its editor: the hue on a spectrum, the strength, the
style, each change applied at once and shown on a line of sample text,
with a reset to the standard pen. The mark popup shows the pens as
swatches in their current look; long-pressing a swatch, or the tune
button, opens the same editor, so a pen can be adjusted while marking.

**How pens paint.** A wash is the ADR 0023 wash at the pen's hue,
faded by strength. An underline is a stroke of a saturated ink at the
pen's hue beneath the covered words, one fourteenth of an em thick,
faded by strength. Both paint behind or beside the runs; the text does
not move.

**A note without a pen.** The popup's last choice is "note only": the
mark holds text and no pen (color index −1). Such a note shows as a
small page icon in the leading above the end of its end verse — after
the verse's last word, in the number color — and the icon is the way
in: tapping it opens the note, while the verse's words stay plain
taps. Its halo matches a footnote marker's. A note-only mark needs
text; the popup's save waits for it. Older app versions read the index
as a wash of pen eight; nothing breaks.

**The notes pane** shows a pen's swatch beside each mark, or the note
icon for a note without one.

## Consequences

- Pens are user data: the op-log carries them, a sync pull repaints
  open views, and a reset is a tombstone like a deleted mark.
- The pen editor and the mark popup share one swatch widget, so what
  the reader picks is what the text shows.
- Word-precision, anchors and the whole-verse rule for other
  translations are untouched; a note icon anchors on the end verse in
  every translation.
- The icon paints into the leading; at the solid line spacing of ADR
  0032 it may touch the descenders of the line above. The alternative
  — space reserved in the typesetting — would reflow the text for
  every note and was rejected under ADR 0023.
